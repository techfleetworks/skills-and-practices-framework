# Practices: The Invisible Components That Drive Empowered Teamwork

## Defining the Recipes for Successful Teamwork

Tech Fleet has built an open source taxonomy of team-based problem solving. We call it the Skills and Practices Framework. With it, we've built a shared language around success. People can understand what it takes to succeed in empowered teamwork. It covers skills, tasks, duties, deliverables, workshops, methods, and many more components.

We've applied this taxonomy to our training experience for members. It lives among onboarding materials, user guides, agile coaching, and training templates. We use it to standardize how we approach team-based problem-solving, and how we speak to it. We're building measurement systems to guide people in practical ways. Now we focus on the components of empowered teams.

## Introducing Team Practices

A lot of people think someone's skills or knowledge affects their success. We believe there's more to empowered teams. We define two contributing factors of empowered teams:

- Empowered teams build common values together.
- Empowered teams build common beliefs and behaviors while working together to achieve outcomes.

We are reframing "soft skills and hard skills" to build future leaders. People-first leaders are the teammates we work with. People-first leaders empower the people around them. They don't just have skills; they hold different mindsets; they behave differently. We call these beliefs and behaviors "team practices".

## This Article Introduces the Basics of Team Practices

We discuss the concepts of values, beliefs, behaviors, and abilities that affect team outcomes. These components apply to any industry and any kind of team-based problem solving.

This article covers:

- The components of strongly performing teams.
- The definition of team practices.
- The differences between team culture, team practices, and team skills.

## How Teams Grow Together

What does it actually take to build empowered teams that produce great work? The Four Stages of Team Development guide us in how to mature strongly performing teams:

- **Forming Stage** — teams get together and start getting to know each other.
- **Storming Stage** — teams get into conflict and start defining how they want to work together.
- **Norming Stage** — teams resolve healthy conflict and align in their work.
- **Performing Stage** — teams are empowered to innovate and perform the work in the ways they want.

The four stages of teamwork are forming, storming, norming and performing. Content credit: MIT https://hr.mit.edu/learning-topics/teams/articles/stages-development. Image credit: Tech Fleet.

## Building Teams that Perform

It's not easy for teams to mature to Performing together. It requires a lot of time and energy for teammates. Teams must build shared values, shared beliefs, and shared behaviors to succeed in outcomes.

How might we measure a team's performance beyond their abilities and their knowledge? We start with breaking down abilities, values, beliefs, and behaviors. Teams can define their shared values together. They can concretely measure beliefs, behaviors, and abilities. Through this they achieve shared outcomes.

Shared team values drive shared beliefs. Shared beliefs drive shared behavior. Abilities and behavior drive shared outcomes. Teams achieve great things when they align in their values, beliefs, and behavior. Image credit: Tech Fleet, and inspired by Amy Edmondson's "Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Environments" (1999).

Here are the foundations of empowered teams:

- **Values** — Teammates align in shared values as a team. The structure of teams and expectations placed on teams affect their values.
- **Beliefs** — Teammates build common beliefs about teamwork. Their beliefs come from their own experiences and perspectives.
- **Behaviors** — Teammates act out their beliefs together through the work. They practice their behaviors while working on their outcomes.
- **Abilities** — Teammates use their skills to perform activities together in the ways that work for them.

With these foundations, empowered teams deliver more value. Their performance and engagement can increase over time.

## Developing as an Empowered Teammate and a People-First Leader

Everyone on an empowered team is a leader. They lead others by empowering and supporting growth. It takes skills and practices to successfully bring yourself to teams. Your knowledge is just the beginning. How you work with others, how you react to unknowns, and how you connect with humans is key. Let's break down the skills and practices you need to be a people-first leader.

### Human-Centered Abilities

Skills are measured abilities that teammates have.

Skills can be technical or interpersonal in nature. Many previous efforts have classified skills to measure skill levels. The adoption of AI in the workplace has evolved current job requirements. Jobs like UX and product will continue to evolve in the abilities they require. As AI comes into focus, people are upskilling in technical areas.

Great teams need more than technical skills. Per the World Economic Forum's 2025 Future of Jobs Report, the skills of the future are human-centered:

- Leadership
- Analytical thinking
- Resilience
- Adaptability
- Empathy
- Self-awareness

The world needs more people with these skills.

### Human-Centered Beliefs and Behaviors

Practices are shared beliefs and behaviors that teammates hold.

Skills and experience aren't enough to succeed. Skilled experts can join a team and fail in working with others. Teams require both skills and practices to succeed.

Practices are more challenging than skills because they involve a lot of human-centered factors:

- Practices require interactions with others.
- Practices require reflection.
- Practices require feedback.
- Practices require mindset shifts.
- Practices require intentional behavior changes.
- Practices require trial and error.

One never becomes an "expert" in practices, unlike skills. They only become more practiced in belief and behavior. People can develop a strong mindset, a strong willingness, and still fail in practice. This makes it challenging for someone to know they are on the right track. Teams should guide in shifting mindsets and behavior. We seek to make the growth of practices more practical for trainees.

## Types of Practices

We've defined seven practices that we think affect empowered team success:

The beliefs and behaviors that affect empowered teamwork are service leadership, agility, empowerment, ownership, psychological safety, continuous improvement, and decision making. Credit: Tech Fleet.

- **Empowerment** — The practice of building power in others.
- **Service Leadership** — The practice of supporting others' growth.
- **Psychological Safety** — The practice of fostering belonging, learning, failure, and challenge.
- **Continuous Improvement** — The practice of incremental growth.
- **Decision Making** — The practice of building consensus with others.
- **Ownership** — The practice of taking and transferring ownership.
- **Agility** — The practice of adaptation and nimbleness.

To get good at these, one must believe in their value. One must practice them through teamwork. These are not something you can only read about. You have to put yourself into practice and do them with others. You have to change habits. You have to build willingness. The entire team must do this in unison.

### Empowerment

Empowerment is the practice of building up others and inspiring them. When people believe in empowerment, they believe everyone on the team brings value. They practice empowerment by encouraging and inspiring others.

### Service Leadership

Service Leadership is the practice of supporting others' growth. When people believe in service leadership, they believe in prioritizing opportunities to learn and grow. They practice service leadership by building empathy with others and checking assumptions before acting.

### Psychological Safety

Psychological Safety is the practice of fostering spaces of belonging, learning, failure, and challenge. When people believe in psychological safety, they believe anyone should try new things and people should challenge the status quo. They practice psychological safety by providing space for multiple perspectives and encouraging experimentation.

### Ownership

Ownership is the practice of committing and of transferring power to others around you. When people believe in ownership, they believe people can succeed even without experience. They practice ownership by allowing others to take over and finishing what they commit to.

### Decision Making

Decision making is the practice of collectively building agreement. When people believe in decision making, they believe that people should decide together and that there are no perfect decisions. They practice decision making by building consensus and asking for feedback before they decide.

### Continuous Improvement

Continuous improvement is the practice of always learning. When people believe in continuous improvement, they believe they will never stop learning. They practice continuous improvement by experimenting with new ways of work and adjusting after failure.

### Agility

Agility is the practice of delivering incremental value into the world. When people believe in agility, they believe they don't need to make perfect decisions and they welcome change. They practice agility by showing work in progress and changing direction when it's warranted.

## How To Measure Skills and Practices

### Measuring Approach

Measuring abilities, beliefs, and behaviors is subjective. It takes reflection. Someone reflects on their own or with others.

The cycle of measuring success in team practices and skills deals with measuring current levels, making a plan, practicing with the team, and reflecting, defining adjustments, then building new measurements. Credit: Tech Fleet.

The measurement cycle is continuous:

1. Measure current levels of beliefs and behavior.
2. Make a plan of action to work on something.
3. Practice with teammates.
4. Reflect on the practice with yourself and others.
5. Define how to adjust.
6. Take new measurements and repeat the cycle.

At a team level, team behaviors can be tracked. Team outcomes can be tracked over time.

### Types of Assessments

We propose to measure beliefs and behaviors through confidence assessments, peer reflections, self reflections, and team outcome tracking. Credit: Tech Fleet.

We seek to move beyond "knowledge assessments" to measure levels of empowered teamwork:

- Measuring team values
- Measuring confidence
- Self-reflections of feedback
- Peer reflections of feedback
- Tracking team outcomes
